AR Ride

AR Ride is an augmented reality navigation heads-up display framework designed for delivery riders in China, aimed at enhancing safety and efficiency in high-risk, fatigue-prone environments. In collaboration with designers from the most popular delivery app, Eleme from Alibaba, we conducted extensive research and interviews with delivery riders, explored AR devices, systematic benchmark regarding existing AR products. Through systematic research and scenario generation in the riding and delivery process, the team developed hi-fi prototypes to showcase their innovative solutions.

background

The Delivery Riders

Delivery riders have become the hottest career choice in recent years. As of 2022, there will be more than 10 million riders nationwide. More and more practitioners in traditional industries such as factory workers, salesmen and individual small businesses choose to become riders. Among them, flexible employment time, high income, and low employment threshold are the main reasons for career choice.

USER RESEARCH

"Why Lives At Risk?"

Shortened delivery times have also caused more accidents in recent years, amid promises of swift service. Delivery drivers said they are putting their lives at risk because of algorithms used by apps to determine the route and travel time allowed before drivers incur a “late delivery” penalty.

Interview

Three delivery riders of multiple background and working modes are interviewed. They’re new rider, full-time rider, and part-time rider who experienced different delivery process with different requirements and troubles.

Existing User Journey

By analyzing the food delivery industry and conducting interviews with Blue Knights, the term for Chinese delivery riders, we identified several issues with existing mobile apps. Our research revealed that the current customer journey consists of five key steps: "Dropping orders, riding to the restaurant, picking up food, riding to the delivery location, and completing the delivery."

design goal

Safety, Efficiency

We aim to address key issues affecting delivery riders in two main areas: safety and efficiency. Unsafe conditions arise from multi-tasking, as riders must manage order information while navigating, making it hard to concentrate on driving. Additionally, the pressure to complete more orders in a limited time leads to dangerous behaviors like speeding and ignoring traffic rules. Complex traffic conditions further complicate their journeys, with unpredictable accidents and sudden obstacles. The small screens mounted on their handlebars require riders to frequently tap and switch pages, increasing risk. Regarding efficiency, 2D navigation struggles with complex road layouts, such as shopping malls and dense urban areas, while riders often waste time searching for addresses, choosing entrances, and waiting for elevators.

Iconic Use Cases

Focusing on the delivery process of riders, we concentrate on three key scenarios: Navigation Guide, Hazard Reminder, and Delivery Guide.

deskrop research

Review of wearable XR devices

Prior to developing our design strategies, we conducted research and reviewed literature and videos on AR technology, with a focus on AR interfaces for heads-up displays (HUDs) and smart glasses or helmets. Additionally, we examined designs, including those of HoloLens 2, the most advanced AR device to date, which effectively integrates environmental perceptions into reality.

Design Principles of AR

Based on our understanding of existing AR technology, we identified four key design principles for AR interfaces: they should be environmentally responsive, support intuitive and flexible interactions, spatially organize virtual content within the physical world, and build upon real-world knowledge and experience, while acknowledging that current AR devices face technical limitations and lack mature, system-level interactions.

Information Guide From AR Products

Prior to starting our design, we analyzed the UX/UI elements of existing AR products, such as Amap AR Navigation, BMW, Apple Maps, Google Maps, and GT7, to refine and optimize our design principles. For instance, in the crucial navigation context, we focused on AutoNavi’s AR navigation, adjusting the information displayed on the AR screen to prioritize key areas within the central line of sight for enhanced visibility.

The Framework of AR Info Display

In terms of information architecture, the matrix illustrates how we divided the 11 scenarios by integrating them into the actual delivery riding process.

The "Physical-Virtual"
Interface Structure

We developed the primary information structure, categorizing it into three levels: resident information (such as time, weather, and order delivery status), responsive UI elements that adapt to the environment (including road navigation, risk warnings, and terminal information guidance), and additional relevant data.

HIfi prototype

On Riding: Basic Status

Regarding UI design, semi-transparent cards were implemented with a frosted glass effect to ensure virtual objects are distinguishable without blocking the real world. For the primary riding scenario, road guidance is prioritized within the central field of vision, using conspicuous blue 3D arrows, while positioning order information and weather updates on either side of the interface.

On Riding: Warning Status

Risk warnings typically arise in riding scenarios, necessitating a focus on safety to ensure riders notice hazards promptly for quick responses.

Scenario1 | Extreme Weather

during a rainstorm, the risk warning in the weather card at the upper left corner is enlarged, complemented by a translucent frosted glass icon to advise riders to slow down, while road navigation adjusts to a speed bump style.

Scenario2 | Complex Road Sections

In complex road sections, like shared streets and turning areas where pedestrians and vehicles mix, virtual street signs are integrated with the real environment, appearing to attach to actual objects such as bus stop signs and building facades.

On Walking

Scenario1 | Approaching Destination

In the walking scenarios, the navigation style remains consistent, emphasizing the customer's specific destination and notifying the rider of any special user requirements when necessary.

Scenario2 | handling multiple orders

When riders are managing multiple orders, they will be informed of which restaurant is ready for pickup and receive immediate guidance to the destination.

conclution

Learn and Reflection

Due to technical limitations, prototyping and user testing were not conducted, and riders could not be invited to experience the product. Unlike conventional screen-based apps, the AR GUI standards and interaction paradigms are still developing, making user testing essential for HUD safety validation.Multi-sensory Design: A key aspect lacking in the project is the incorporation of sound and haptic feedback. One of the main advantages of AR/VR experiences is their multi-sensory nature, providing designers with a new dimension beyond graphics. In this project, sound and haptics could serve as indicators and reminders for riders facing potential dangers or notifications.
